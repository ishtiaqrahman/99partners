# Acquisition workbench — product and engineering handoff

## 1. Product decision

Build a local-first acquisition screening app for an investment associate preparing a partner review. The associate should be able to find a target, explain the evidence for its priority, assemble a three-company investment case, and defend the result when a partner changes an assumption. The signature interaction is **open a winning trio → challenge its budget or valuation assumptions → see exactly which companies move and why**.

The employer's brief asks for runnable code, four CSVs, a one-page README, and a live walkthrough. It gives an expected maximum effort of about two hours and a submission window of 24 hours from receipt. The candidate wants to invest additional effort in a productized experience. Plan development by acceptance gates. Track and report actual time, including planning and AI assistance. Label the dashboard and scenario features as candidate extensions. Check the actual deadline independently.

### What the data establishes

- The PDF names v8 with 960 rows. The supplied v9 has 1,000 rows, 10 columns, 1,000 distinct four-character reference IDs, and no company names. Use v9. Its SHA-256 is `06dec7d3196e862a7de7b3b2ff2c3d25edd6b949974801c56c8d1eca76afe6c5`. A changed input starts a new run. Use IDs such as **Account 0004** throughout.
- There are 167 raw industry labels and 269 distinct descriptions after normalizing case and whitespace. Recurring customer and workflow phrases make a small, inspectable vocabulary practical.
- 886 rows contain a revenue figure. Of these, 385 have an explicit currency marker such as CAD, US$, or £. The other **501** include 82 plain dollar signs and 419 amounts without a currency marker. Among the observed formats are 81 ranges, 92 bare decimals, and 75 bare integers. The four indicators of missing revenue account for the other 114 rows.
- The profitability field includes 118 occurrences of “Yes,” 85 of “Profitable,” and 115 of “Cash-flow positive.” Ownership includes 66 “Bank term loan only (no equity investors)” and 63 “Government grants / SR&ED credits only; founder-owned.” These distinctions can materially change the shortlist.
- The counts above are input observations. Tier totals, winning trios, runtimes, and scenario changes must be measured by the implementation.

## 2. Adversarial review of the first plan

| Challenge | What breaks | Decision and verification |
|---|---|---|
| A partner asks where the USD figure came from. | A currency default on 501 records looks like fact once amounts are formatted. A Toronto HQ can report in USD and a UK HQ can have an unmarked range. | Store the raw token, scale, currency basis, FX rate, and low/base/high amounts. Show the assumption beside the value. Provide an explicit-currency filter and an evidence-only sensitivity check. |
| An analyst searches for attractive dental software. | The same buyer can purchase a practice system, a birthday-card tool, or consulting. A single “dental” or “VMS” tag loses the distinction. | Separate buyer, product role, and evidence. Test actual rows 0012 and 0034 alongside a core product and a horizontal product. |
| An interviewer asks why a profitable family-owned company is B. | Looking only for numeric EBITDA misses positive earnings expressed in words. Looking for “funding” can misclassify debt and grants as equity. | Publish an explicit mapping and reason codes. Verify 0004 and 0019 as positive controls. Use 0002 and 0013 to exercise financing and acquisition distinctions. |
| The recommendation appears more certain than the data. | A peer estimate can enter its own peer set, or an inferred revenue can be mistaken for observed annual revenue. | Separate observed and estimated fields. Use a fixed, observed-only peer pool. Every estimated figure shows method, peer count, and role in eligibility. |
| The highest-revenue trio consists of cheap add-ons. | Product-role multiples influence the optimizer. Financial winners might rank low for outreach. | Keep both tiers available for grouping. Show tier mix and member multiples. Add a comparison against a uniform software multiple and a clearly labeled core-only scenario. Keep the official financial objective intact. |

Adjacent buyers such as food processors and food distributors can be distinct when the buyer definitions are documented. |
| A reviewer recomputes the top ten. | Greedy selection, pruning to high-revenue targets, rounding millions before comparison, requiring ten disjoint portfolios, or ordering only by revenue changes answers. | Use one exact, readable query, integer cents, a deterministic total order, and an independent exhaustive oracle on small datasets. |
| Ten winning rows look like ten different opportunities. | Several may share two members. Showing ten standalone cards hides concentration. | Expose shared members and show how many of the ten include each account. Allow overlap across groups and document the interpretation. |
| The user asks for “Canadian accounts under 10M.” | A query box might choose revenue over valuation silently, treat unknown as negative, or overwrite existing filters. | Show an editable filter preview. Ask which monetary field is meant. Preserve existing filters until the user applies a valid interpretation. |
| The reviewer changes the budget during the interview. | Cached results, previously exported official files, and a live scenario can disagree without explanation. | Give each result an input and policy identity. Scenarios have their own parameters and labels. Compare to the official baseline and test that its files retain their original hashes. |
| The dashboard is polished but hard to operate or rerun. | A large table, invented company names, noisy badges, a remote model, or several independently deployed services defeat the demo. | Use the official IDs; paginate, support keyboard operation, keep a single local launch command, and run the core workflow offline after dependency installation. |
| “Well documented” becomes comments on every line. | Duplicated explanations drift between UI, code, and README. The candidate struggles to find the actual decision rule. | Document the business decisions beside the implementation. Generate the Method view and explanations from the same rule metadata. Put one “change this if…” note at each major policy boundary. |

Keep the first plan's strongest elements: the account evidence panel, separate outreach and grouping decisions, fixed exercise FX rates, independent optimizer verification, a private local run, and a reproducible CLI. Invest the extra effort in the path an analyst will use from start to finish.

## 3. The experience to build

### Accounts — find a target and prepare a diligence question

- Open directly on a useful, populated table. Provide saved views **All accounts**, **Priority outreach**, and **Needs financial review** with live counts. Keep auto-generated evidence tags separate from optional analyst tags such as “Discuss with partner.” Store user-created views and tags locally, keyed to dataset identity. They should never change official tiers.
- Show ID, customer vertical, product role, tier, annual revenue, and estimated valuation as the primary table columns. Support sorting with stable ID ties and nulls last. Put ownership, earnings, country, evidence, and tags in filters. Show how many of the total match. Offer reset and a clear empty state.
- Clicking a row opens a side panel with the tier reason, quoted source description, a short revenue-to-valuation calculation, and one evidence-linked diligence question. For 0002, a good question concerns the currency and point in its 2–4M range. For 0005, it concerns ownership. Give each account a shareable local deep link and restore table position when closing it.
- Offer natural language as an alternate way to configure the existing filters. Support tier, country, customer vertical, role, earnings evidence, evidence tags, monetary thresholds, and sorting. Example: “Tier A in Canada, valuation under $15M, highest revenue first.” Display the interpretation first. For “under $15M,” offer **Revenue** or **Valuation**. For unsupported concepts such as churn, identify the missing field. Numbers in a query operate in USD, visibly labeled. Define how new filters merge and how conflicts are resolved.
- Implement a deterministic parser for documented phrases, aliases, numbers, and sort directions. Return a typed filter object plus any unresolved spans. Manual controls and parsed requests share a validator and query executor. No vector index or external model is needed for the provided data. A later model front end can use the same contract.

### Groups — make the partner-review moment work

- Lead with the top trio: three compact member rows showing buyer, revenue, multiple, and valuation, plus totals and headroom. Put the other nine in a ranked table. Opening a member should land on its evidence panel. A simple budget bar has labeled values and a textual equivalent.
- Pin the official base case beside one scenario. Ship **budget** first, then a **uniform software multiple** preset and an **explicit currency only** preset. Add a core-products-only filter once the comparisons work. Keep each change reversible and explicit. Users should be able to demonstrate one change at a time.
- On **Run scenario**, show which members entered, left, or stayed, the change in revenue, and whether the original winner still qualifies. Give a factual explanation such as “original trio exceeds the new cap by $X” or “still eligible; another trio has higher revenue under the revised model.” Handle equal revenue by naming the tie-break. Distinguish a changed eligibility filter from a changed price. If fewer than ten trios qualify, display the actual number. Zero results should explain the active constraints and preserve the baseline.
- For range-based inputs, show what happens to the selected trio if high endpoints replace midpoints. Label this a sensitivity calculation. The app may call a trio optimal under that changed policy only after rerunning the search over the full eligible pool.

### Method — let the interviewer audit the system

- Use a simple accessible architecture diagram with clickable stages: **source → normalize → classify → value → optimize → export**. A selected stage shows its inputs, outputs, rule location, and a real example. Include a text version and make every essential action usable by keyboard.
- Show the active FX table, tier gates, multiples, tie-breaks, input version, coverage, and eligibility waterfall. Publish counts based on the current run with mutually exclusive exclusion reasons so the waterfall reconciles.
- Place the four official downloads here. In all three views, show whether the user is looking at the baseline, a filtered view, or a recalculated scenario. Display precise amounts on demand wherever formatting could conceal the strict budget boundary.
- Choose a restrained visual system: warm white, charcoal type, one teal action color, amber for review; tabular numerals, aligned USD columns, compact row spacing, visible focus, and human-readable text for every status. On narrow screens stack the evidence panel under the selected record. Prioritize stable layouts, fast back navigation, useful empty states, and readable numbers.

## 4. Analytical contract

### Input, normalization, and evidence

1. Read IDs and raw fields as strings. Validate required columns, nonblank unique IDs, and row reconciliation. Retain original columns. Keep a source row reference. Duplicate IDs block export and produce a useful error; repeated descriptions are expected.
2. Parse money with decimal arithmetic. An explicit denomination wins. Use USD at 1.00, CAD at 0.70, EUR at 1.10, GBP at 1.30, AUD at 0.65, and MXN at 0.05. For this fixture use a documented USD assumption for bare $ and unmarked amounts; retain separate basis flags. Explicit scale wins. On v9, bare fractions/small decimals from 0.8 through 60.5 are interpreted as millions, while the bare large integers represent units. Document the supported pattern and flag unfamiliar scales for review. Preserve both endpoints of ranges, use their midpoint in the base case, and retain approximation flags. Keep fixed-point integer cents for decisions and explicit rounding at the money conversion boundary.
3. Leave marked missing revenue null in the observed field. Unexpected nonempty formats require review; expose their count independently of declared missing values. Negative amounts, contradictory units, invalid ranges, and unfamiliar currencies must be surfaced. A new format must never quietly become a normal USD amount.
4. Normalize country conservatively using city and region together. Treat standalone ambiguous locations as unknown. Preserve rebrand text and use the original founding year where clear. Preserve employee ranges. Record age bands as supplied; founder age plays no part in tiering, eligibility, or valuation.
5. Define a buyer vocabulary with canonical IDs, labels, and matched phrases. Classify product role separately as core vertical workflow, vertical add-on, horizontal software, services/hardware, or unresolved. Explicit consulting and horizontal phrases take precedence over broad industry labels. Bare “software” or “SaaS” does not prove a core workflow. Keep matched text. Flag conflicting evidence for review. Never key decisions to individual account IDs.

### Priority and valuation

- **Tier A** requires a confirmed customer vertical, core operational software, positive earnings evidence, and independent ownership evidence under the screening thesis. Positive profitability self-reports (“Yes” in the profitability field or “Profitable”), positive EBITDA, and positive net income qualify with distinct evidence labels. Cash-flow-positive alone receives its own state. Accept explicit founder/family/self-funded language, no outside investors, and explicit debt-only or grant-only independent cases. “Private,” minority investment, venture backing, acquired, and subsidiary each retain a distinct label. A failure of the screening gate does not establish that an acquisition is impossible.
- **Tier B** includes the balance. Emit one short reason per company, naming the decisive evidence or missing fact. Use deterministic gate order: buyer/product fit, independence, then earnings. Preserve all reason codes for the detail panel. Unit-test the thesis against real and synthetic examples. Record its conservative bias.
- **Valuation** uses a disclosed screening assumption of 3× revenue for core software, 2× for add-ons and horizontal software, 1× for services/hardware, and a provisional 2× for unresolved business models. Present as estimated enterprise value. These multiples lack market calibration. Put them in one policy structure. A uniform 2× software scenario isolates how the role premiums affect the result.
- When revenue is missing, estimate it for valuation using the median of at least five observed peers with the same canonical buyer and role; fall back to the role median with the same minimum, then the overall observed median. Report method and sample size. Unknown buyers cannot form a same-buyer peer group. Calculate peer sets first from observed rows, so imputed values never feed another estimate. If the entire input lacks observed revenue, return a clear unmet prerequisite. Keep `observed_revenue` and `revenue_used_for_valuation` separate.
- **Official grouping pool:** both tiers; software with a confirmed customer vertical, a positive annual revenue parsed from the input, and a positive valuation. Core systems and vertical add-ons qualify. Horizontal products lack a distinct buyer vertical under the brief; services-only firms lack a software vertical. Missing annual revenue prevents comparison on the stated objective. This is a candidate interpretation; report its effects. An explicitly marked sensitivity can include peer-estimated revenue. A record with an assumed currency or a range midpoint remains eligible in the baseline with visible flags.

### Exact ranking

Use an embedded DuckDB query over the eligible, typed account table. A three-way join with ordered IDs `a < b < c` gives each unordered trio one representation. Enforce all three pairwise vertical inequalities. Require the integer-cent sum of valuations to be strictly below the budget. Order by combined annual revenue descending, valuation ascending, then the three ordered IDs ascending. Return up to ten distinct trios. The same company can appear in different trios. Grouping categories, currency policy, eligibility, ranking, and tie-breaks must appear in Method.

At 1,000 accounts there are 166,167,000 potential unordered triples before exclusions. Measure full-data latency, peak memory, and result equivalence. Start with one readable query and a narrow table of IDs, verticals, valuations, and revenue. Apply individual or pair valuation pruning only where positive valuations make it safe. Inspect the query plan if timing is poor. Cache baseline and repeated scenarios by dataset, policy, budget, and eligibility filters. Never reduce the financial search to an arbitrary shortlist for speed. Aim for subsecond table interactions and a few seconds for a fresh scenario on the interview machine. Report measured timings.

## 5. Architecture and delivery contracts

Use **one Python analysis package**, a **local FastAPI process**, and a **React/TypeScript dashboard built with Vite**. DuckDB runs in process. The extra UI layer earns its keep in the evidence drawer, keyboard-accessible filter chips, linked navigation, and side-by-side scenario comparison. In the packaged run the Python server serves the compiled frontend; Node is needed only to develop or rebuild the UI. Keep all assets local and bind to loopback by default.

| Boundary | Owns | Implementation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis package | Normalized accounts, policy metadata, provenance, tiers, valuations, exact grouping | Pure or narrowly scoped functions. Enforce the same money, eligibility, and ordering conventions across CLI, downloads, and scenarios. |
| CLI and exports | Input validation, baseline generation, four official CSVs, run identity | Build outputs into a temporary run directory, validate, then expose the completed run. Preserve the prior complete run on failure. Use deterministic column and row order. |
| Local HTTP API | Typed account queries, account details, official results, scenario calculation, method metadata, downloads | Bind query parameters. Validate fields, operators, budgets, and sort keys. Keep the scenario path read-only with respect to official output files. |
| React UI | Interaction state, query previews, tables, evidence, comparisons, accessibility | Render business decisions supplied by Python. Preserve exact amounts in detail views. Keep manual tags and saved views in browser storage with dataset identity. |

Suggested source layout: `src/workbench/policy.py` for declarative mappings, `cleaning.py` for parsing, `analysis.py` for classification/tiers/valuation, `groups.sql` for the optimizer, `pipeline.py` for orchestration and exports, `api.py` for routes, `filters.py` for query interpretation, `web/` for the interface and `tests/` for fixtures and checks. Split a module further only when it has a clear second responsibility. Use one entry point for generating artifacts and one for serving the app. Avoid background job infrastructure unless measurements establish a need.

**API behaviors.** Accounts accepts validated filters, sorts, pagination, and optional ID/text search. Account detail returns raw evidence and each calculation step. Query preview returns structured filters and ambiguity or unsupported-text messages. Group calculation accepts an explicit scenario object and returns parameters, eligibility counts, trios, contributions, and comparison with the baseline. Method returns rule descriptions and coverage from the same policy objects used by the engine. Downloads identify the official run. Unknown fields and malformed parameters produce actionable validation errors.

| Required artifact | Minimum acceptance contract |
|---|---|
| Cleaned dataset | All original IDs and fields, normalized fields, canonical buyer and product role, source evidence, revenue basis and quality flags. Exactly one row per input ID. |
| A/B list | ID, A or B, short human-readable reason. Exactly one row per input ID. Detailed evidence remains available in the cleaned file and UI. |
| Valuations | ID, observed USD revenue where available, valuation revenue basis, imputation method and peer count when applicable, multiple, estimated USD enterprise value. One estimate per account for v9. |
| Top ten groups | Rank, three ordered IDs and buyer verticals, combined annual revenue, combined estimated valuation, budget headroom. Use exact, machine-readable USD amounts. All totals must reconcile to the per-account exports. |

Proposed reviewer commands: `make setup`, `make analyze INPUT=/path/to/candidate_companies_v9.csv`, `make test`, `make demo`. Document equivalent direct commands where needed. Pin tested dependencies. The analytics path runs independently of the web build. Package the ZIP with the four outputs, code, one-page README, locally built UI, and permitted source data or explicit input-path instructions. Keep the private planning handoff outside the employer-facing ZIP. Leave submission to the candidate.

## 7. Implementation sequence with decision gates

1. **Freeze the contract.** Record input identity, required outputs, intended policy, and interpretation of grouping. Write the small boundary fixtures. Prepare a one-page README outline and time log. Exit when the intended answers for edge cases can be checked by hand.
2. **Deliver an end-to-end slice.** Start with explicit currency, one core product, one add-on, one horizontal product, and the optimizer fixture. Run input → four outputs → one dashboard row and its detail panel. This tests ID and money types across every boundary before expanding the taxonomy.
3. **Cover the real file.** Implement all observed revenue forms, missingness, location/ownership/earnings distinctions, buyer and role rules, valuations, cohort fallback, and reason codes. Review samples across roles and unresolved cases; inspect winners and accounts near the priority boundary. Publish coverage and evidence. Preserve unresolved rows honestly.
4. **Prove the official answer.** Run independent oracle comparisons, full-data integration checks, and performance measurement. Reconcile files and totals. Ship the CLI, reproducible outputs, and usable one-page README at this gate. The core submission should be reviewable here.
5. **Deliver the analyst workflow.** Finish the responsive Accounts table, filters, saved views, drawer, and Groups table. Add the Method diagram and CSV downloads. Verify back-navigation and agreement among UI and exports.
6. **Build the partner-review interaction.** Compare official and changed budgets first. Add the multiple and evidence scenarios, membership differences, ties, empty states, and member overlap. Redirect extra UI effort here until the workflow is convincing end to end.
7. **Finish natural-language filtering and polish.** Cover a documented vocabulary, editable previews, unsupported queries, and a small evaluation set. Target full accuracy on documented examples plus correct ambiguity handling. Use actual results to choose three useful example queries. Add optional analyst tags, visual refinements, accessibility, and measured responsiveness.
8. **Rehearse and package.** Ask another person, or use a clean environment, to follow the reviewer instructions. Rehearse one policy edit and one optimizer boundary check with the actual code. Capture honest AI use, actual time, limitations, and next steps. Package privately. Keep a tested local demo available for the call.

### Documentation standard

Every decision-bearing module starts with a short orientation: what it receives, what it decides, and where that decision appears in the product. Each public analytical function documents units, null treatment, assumptions, and the next consumer. Put brief comments beside the actual business choices, including strict inequality, imputation eligibility, debt versus equity, and tie-break order. Pair each consequential rule with one small example or named test. Use ordinary control names and data fields in the UI so the candidate can move from a label to the corresponding policy. Keep the employer-facing README to one page covering how to run, method, assumptions, AI use, actual time, and future work.

### Five-minute demonstration

Start with “Which accounts merit a call, and which combinations fit our underwriting assumptions?” Open 0004 and trace its source text to its tier and valuation. Contrast it with 0001 or 0012. Ask a supported natural-language question and show the interpreted filters. Open the actual highest-revenue eligible trio and identify its most consequential assumption. Change that assumption in the comparison, explain the measured outcome even if the same trio wins, and show where the policy lives in Method. Finish by opening the reproducible CSV for the official result. Keep a test ready to demonstrate that exactly $30M fails.
